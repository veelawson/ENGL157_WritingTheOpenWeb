at://engl157/about

What this course is about

Rhetoric is the study of how communication shapes what people believe and do. Many people associate the word with political speeches or empty talk, but scholars of rhetoric study persuasion wherever it happens, including in images and in the design of the tools we use every day.

This course applies that lens to social media. We'll look at platform rhetorics, the ways an interface's defaults and algorithms encourage some behaviors and discourage others, and at rhetorics of code, the arguments that software and its documentation make about who users are and what they ought to be able to do. You don't need any background in rhetoric or programming to take part, since we'll build that vocabulary together over the first weeks of the semester.

Our case study is the AT Protocol, an open protocol that Bluesky made famous but that now supports community-run networks like Blacksky and experimental apps like Anisota, each with its own idea of what social media should be. Because so much of that ecosystem is developed in the open, we can read its specifications alongside the debates developers have about them, and our own writing can join those conversations while they are still unfolding.

at://engl157/atmosphere

Experiments across the network

The people building on the AT Protocol rarely agree about what a social network is for. Some communities are running and governing their own infrastructure, while independent developers question the endless scroll or build tools that have little to do with posting at all. These are a few of the projects we might study together, and the list will grow as you bring your own finds.

Community-governed infrastructure

Blacksky

A community-governed network for Black users that runs its own app and moderation on an independent implementation of the protocol.

Northsky

A worker-owned nonprofit cooperative, hosted in Canada, building a safe and sustainable platform for 2SLGBTQIA+ and other marginalized communities.

Rethinking the feed

Anisota

A card-based interface and game designed for more mindful use, where you flip through a finite deck of posts until your stamina runs out and like counts give way to trading-card rarity. It also builds writing support into posting, from a drafting adviser to alt-text help.

Graze

A feed builder that lets anyone design the algorithm deciding what they and their community see.

Writing and research

Leaflet

Collaborative documents and long-form publishing, stored in each writer's own account data.

Semble

A social knowledge network where researchers collect sources and see how others connect them.

Gathering and building

Roomy

An open-source, Discord-style space for community conversation.

Tangled

Git collaboration on the protocol, where your code and contributions travel with your identity.

These projects are run by their own communities and developers, and including them here is an invitation to study them.

Questions we'll take up

What values does a piece of infrastructure encode, and who gets to decide?

Why does the history of the web keep swinging between openness and enclosure?

How does an interface persuade us, and what would it take to design one that persuades differently?

How can technical writers advocate for users inside systems they didn't design?

The arc of the semester

Weeks 1–3

Infrastructure as Rhetoric

An introduction to rhetoric and to the idea that platforms make arguments through their design, shaping what users and communities are able to do.

Weeks 4–6

Histories of the Open Web

From email and Usenet to the era of centralized platforms, with attention to why openness keeps giving way to enclosure.

Weeks 7–10

ATProto as Case Study

Hands-on time in the AT Protocol ecosystem along with conversations with developers who build on it. You'll also begin scoping your project.

Weeks 11–15

Applied Projects

You'll develop your project through peer review and revision, then share it publicly at the end of the term.

at://engl157/project

What you'll make

Your major project is a piece of professional writing for a real ecosystem. Depending on your interests, that might be a guide for new users, documentation for a developer tool, a UX audit of an app built on the protocol, or a small contribution to an open-source project.

Projects are scoped to your technical comfort, and the emphasis stays on audience and rhetorical choices. The finished project can also serve as a writing sample when you apply for jobs or graduate programs.

Who this course is for

English and PTW students

If you study Professional and Technical Writing or another area of English, this course extends the rhetorical analysis you already practice to the platforms and code that shape so much contemporary writing. You'll also gain experience writing for developer audiences and working within open-source communities. No programming background is expected, only curiosity about how these systems work.

CS and technical majors

If you study computer science or a related field, this course offers a humanistic perspective on technical work. We'll ask what a protocol assumes about its users and how documentation can widen or narrow who gets to participate, and you'll practice explaining technical systems to readers who don't share your background.

Questions

What is rhetoric, and do I need to have studied it?

Rhetoric is the study of how communication shapes belief and action. You don't need any prior coursework in it, since the first weeks of the semester introduce the concepts we'll use all term.

Do I need to know how to code?

No. Some projects involve technical work for students who want it, and every project can be completed through careful research and writing.

Is the course in person?

Yes. We meet on campus Mondays and Wednesdays from 10:30 to 11:45 AM, and guest developers join over Zoom while we're together in the classroom.

How do I enroll?

Search for ENGL 157 in MySJSU when Spring 2027 enrollment opens. There is one section this semester, so it will be the only ENGL 157 listed.

Who can I ask about the course?
